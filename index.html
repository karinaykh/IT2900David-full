Leadership Coaching Simulation

Supporting David

📋 Scenario Overview

You are a mentor at a software company: a senior software engineer in another team. The company's mentoring programme paired you with David when he joined, four months ago, straight after university.

Note: David asked to meet earlier than your regular schedule, which is unusual for him. He seemed less enthusiastic than usual, though nothing was said outright.

👥 People and projects

David

  • Your mentee, a software engineer
  • Joined four months ago as a fresh graduate
  • Works on the reporting system, in Eliza's team
  • In your first two meetings, he was upbeat and full of questions

Eliza

  • David's team lead

The banking app

  • The company's high-profile client project, for its biggest client, a major bank
  • When David joined, he asked you a lot about it

The reporting system

  • The internal system David works on
  • It produces the client reports overnight

🗓️ How This Activity Works

The activity has four parts. You talk with David in parts 1 and 3, and with 🧠 your Inner Voice, your own thinking, in parts 2 and 4. David does not hear what you tell your Inner Voice.

1
Meeting with David

👤 Talking with David

David speaks first. After that, you lead the conversation.

2
Pause to think

🧠 Talking to your Inner Voice

Partway through, David pauses for a moment. Your Inner Voice asks you a few questions about the meeting so far.

3
Meeting continues

👤 Talking with David

You decide when the meeting ends. Close it as you would in real life, for example by thanking him and saying goodbye.

4
Your reflection

🧠 Talking to your Inner Voice

After the meeting, your Inner Voice asks you a few questions about how you coached David.

  • This is your third meeting with David.
  • Set aside about 40 minutes to do this in one sitting, without breaks.
  • You have one attempt.
  • The chat does not end when you say goodbye to David: your reflection starts. The chat ends after your last answer.
  • If the chat stays open after your reflection has ended, click Submit activity.
  • Your feedback report appears on screen a few minutes after the chat ends.

🎓 Learning Objectives

▼

By completing this simulation, you will:

  • Practice building psychological safety through empathetic listening
  • Apply coaching techniques to help someone discover their own insights
  • Learn to resist the urge to give immediate solutions
  • Develop skills in facilitating reflection and self-discovery
  • Experience how trust affects the depth of conversation
  • Understand the difference between solving FOR someone vs. helping them solve

When you're ready, click the 'Start' button to begin your conversation with David.
